By Chris Howerton, Ph.D.
Executive Summary

The midterm elections set the rhythm of the week, but the biggest move came from outside healthcare. Biotech slipped on Monday and rose on Tuesday and Wednesday, as Democrats won control of both chambers of Congress and hospital, insurer, and medtech stocks rallied. On Thursday a sell-off led by AI and chip stocks took the S&P 500 down 4.4% and the Nasdaq down 5.5%, and biotech fell harder than either: the XBI dropped 6.7% and the median biotech 6.5%, before Friday recovered part of the loss. For the week the XBI fell 2.4% and IBB 1.8%, while the XHE rose 2.9% on the strength of its post-election Wednesday. The smallest biotechs again fared worst, with the micro-cap median down 3.2% against 0.6% for mid/large caps, and for a second week commercial-stage biotechs did not outperform development-stage peers; this week they trailed in every size tier. Company news still moved individual stocks. Inovio's FDA approval more than tripled its share price in a day and sent Precigen, whose therapy treats the same rare disease, down 26%; quarterly results lifted TransMedics, Medline, and Xeris and sank iRhythm and Stevanato. Capital markets stayed open but selective, with follow-on offerings for Inovio, Orchestra BioMed, and Annovis Bio.

01 · Macro View

Sector Performance by Market Cap

Rates were a minor factor this week: the 10-year Treasury yield eased from 4.94% on October 30 to 4.84% on November 5 (FRED, 11/5). The week turned on politics and then on technology stocks. Day by day, the median biotech fell 1.8% on Monday, rose 1.3% on Tuesday and 1.7% on Wednesday, fell 6.5% on Thursday, and rose 3.1% on Friday. Medtech's Wednesday was stronger: the median medtech rose 3.5%, the small and mid/large medtech medians rose 5.9% and 5.4%, and the XHE gained 7.6%. For the week, biotech medians fell 3.2% in micro caps, 1.5% in small caps, and 0.6% in mid/large caps, and only 34% of micro-caps finished higher. Within each biotech size tier, commercial-stage companies trailed development-stage companies (down 4.8% versus up 0.3% in small caps, down 2.0% versus up 1.6% in mid/large caps, and down 4.1% versus down 3.2% in micro caps). Medtech split by size: the micro-cap median fell 2.7% while the mid/large median rose 1.1%.

02 · Market Movers

Top Gainers & Laggards

Gainers

Inovio PharmaceuticalsINO +101.7%+$0.06B

Rose 214% on November 2, the first trading day after the FDA granted accelerated approval on October 30 to VOCERYQ, a DNA immunotherapy that reduces the frequency of surgery in adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) (Inovio 8-K, 10/30). The stock had fallen 22% on October 30, before the decision was announced, and gave back part of Monday's gain over the rest of the week, including after the company priced a public offering of about $75M at $1.05 per share on November 5 (Inovio 424B5, 11/5).

TransMedics GroupTMDX +33.7%+$0.77B

Rose 28% on November 6 after reporting record third-quarter revenue of $203.0M, up 41%, and record case volume. It raised its 2026 revenue guidance, excluding its PAD Aviation charter services, to $750M to $760M from $737M to $757M (TransMedics 8-K, 11/5).

MedlineMDLN +23.4%

Rose 18% on November 4 after reporting third-quarter net sales of $7.9B, up 10.4%, raising its 2026 organic sales growth outlook to 10.0% to 10.5% from 9.0% to 10.0%, and disclosing a $1B share repurchase authorization. The quarter included an $81M tariff refund benefit (Medline 8-K, 11/4). Health care stocks rose broadly the same day after the midterm elections (Trefis, 11/5).

Xeris BiopharmaXERS +41.9%+$0.82B

Rose 25% on November 5, the day the XBI fell 6.7%, and another 16% on November 6, after reporting third-quarter revenue of $107.2M, up 44%, with Recorlev net revenue up 93% to $71.5M. The company raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $395M to $405M and its 2030 revenue outlook to more than $1B from about $750M (Xeris 8-K, 11/5).

Laggards

PrecigenPGEN −35.6%−$0.88B

Fell 26% on November 2 after Inovio's VOCERYQ was approved for RRP, the disease treated by Precigen's PAPZIMEOS, which was approved in August 2025 and accounts for nearly all of Precigen's revenue (TIKR, 11/2). On November 4 Precigen reported third-quarter PAPZIMEOS net revenue of $72.9M, up 37% from the second quarter, and positive operating cash flow a quarter earlier than it had guided (Precigen 8-K, 11/4). The stock fell another 16% on November 5, the day of the market-wide sell-off.

Stevanato GroupSTVN −30.0%−$1.76B

Fell 29% on November 3 after third-quarter results. Revenue rose 8% to €326.1M and the drug containment and delivery supplier kept its 2026 guidance, citing growth in non-GLP-1 biologics that offset lower GLP-1 revenue (Stevanato release, 11/3). The company also said new orders ended the quarter below its targets and that it expects low single-digit revenue growth in 2027 (IBTimes, 11/3).

iRhythm HoldingsIRTC −26.9%−$1.07B

Fell 31% on November 6 after third-quarter results. Revenue rose 14.9% to $221.6M and the company raised its 2026 revenue guidance to $890M to $895M, but its net loss widened to $29.9M, including $49.8M of litigation settlement charges (iRhythm 8-K, 11/5). The company also said it no longer plans to submit its Zio MCT monitor to the FDA this year and now expects to file in 2027 (Seeking Alpha, 11/6; RTTNews, 11/6).

04 · Strategic Themes

The Week's Narrative

An Election Bounce, Then a Sell-Off

The week's path was set first by the November 3 midterms and then by a market-wide reversal. Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate, and on Wednesday several hospital and insurer stocks rose about 9% to 13% on expectations of a slowdown or reversal in healthcare spending cuts (CNBC, 11/4). In our universe the median medtech rose 3.5% that day and the median biotech 1.7%.

Thursday reversed it. A sell-off led by AI and semiconductor stocks took the S&P 500 down 4.4% and the Nasdaq Composite down 5.5% (FRED, 11/5; Yahoo Finance, 11/5). Biotech fell further than the broad market: the XBI dropped 6.7% and the biotech median 6.5%, with every size tier down between 5.3% and 7.5%. Friday recovered part of the decline.

CSO's TakeThis week biotech moved with the market more than with its own news. The election produced a relief rally in health care that was strongest in hospitals, insurers, and medtech, and Thursday's sell-off began in technology stocks but took biotech down further than the S&P 500. Across the week the smallest biotechs fell most, in line with the risk-off pattern of recent editions. For the second week running, commercial-stage companies did not hold up better than development-stage peers, which complicates the rotation toward commercial-stage names we described earlier this fall.

Technology Cools, and Healthcare's Link to It Is an Open Question

The Nasdaq Composite closed at its 2026 high on October 6 and has since fallen 10.9% (FRED). Over the same span the XBI rose 0.9%, IBB 5.2%, and XHE 5.1%. Measured week to week, biotech and the Nasdaq have moved together only loosely: the correlation of weekly XBI and Nasdaq returns is 0.44 since December 2025 and 0.15 over the last 13 weeks. Measured day to day, the link has tightened. Since late September the correlation of daily returns has been 0.67, up from 0.50 earlier in the year, driven by sharp down days such as Thursday.

Dealmaking has been a support for the sector through the year. Biopharma M&A reached $130B in the first half of 2026, nearly matching 2025's full-year total of $133B (IQVIA, 7/2026). In our conversations with bankers and investors this week, the tone on M&A was constructive.

CSO's TakeThe data do not yet settle whether a cooling technology trade helps or hurts healthcare. Since the Nasdaq's peak, biotech and medtech indexes have held their level while technology fell, which is consistent with money moving toward healthcare; but on the sharpest down days biotech has fallen alongside the broad market, and this week by more. We are treating it as an open question.
