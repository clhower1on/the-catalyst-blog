Executive Summary

A market-wide sell-off set the week, and the smallest biotechs took the brunt of it. A week after Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as CEO, selling that began in AI and chip stocks took the S&P 500 down 8.6% and the Nasdaq Composite down 10.0%, leaving the S&P 500 more than 10% below its October 6 record. Healthcare indexes fell less than the broad market, with the XBI down 6.9%, IBB 6.1%, and XHE 5.8%, but the typical stock fell further than the indexes: the median biotech lost 10.4%, and the micro-cap median 14.2% against 6.8% for mid/large caps. Rates were not the driver, since the 10-year Treasury yield fell during the week. After two weeks in which commercial-stage biotechs did not hold up better than development-stage peers, they did again: among small and mid/large caps the commercial-stage median fell between 4.4 and 4.7 percentage points less, although among micro caps stage made no difference. Company news still moved individual stocks. Summit Therapeutics fell 30% after an FDA complete response letter and Quanterix 33% after its CEO stepped down, while Insmed rose 21% in a day on a report that Sanofi is weighing a bid. Lilly agreed to buy the preclinical Calytrix Bio for up to $1.5B. Private rounds continued, led by Science Corp's $400M, and we found no life sciences IPO pricing during the week.

01 · Macro View

Sector Performance by Market Cap

The week's losses came on the first and last days. The XBI fell 3.1% on Monday and 2.7% on Friday, and between them dropped less than 1% a day. Every biotech size tier followed the same path, and the micro-cap median fell most on both days: 4.0% on Monday and 5.1% on Friday, against 2.6% and 2.2% for mid/large caps. For the week, biotech medians fell 14.2% in micro caps, 9.3% in small caps, and 6.8% in mid/large caps. Medtech showed the same ordering by size, with the micro-cap median down 15.1%, small caps 7.3%, and mid/large caps 3.8%. The 10-year Treasury yield eased from 5.15% on November 13 to 4.92% on November 20 (FRED; U.S. Treasury, 11/20), so lower rates did not cushion the sector. Large pharma did not provide shelter either: Eli Lilly fell 6.7%, the week's largest dollar decline in our universe at about $91B of market value by our count, and Merck fell 9.5%.

02 · Market Movers

Top Gainers & Laggards

Gainers

InsmedINSM +16.6%+$4.4B

Rose 21% on November 19 after Bloomberg reported that Sanofi is weighing an acquisition of the company, whose bronchiectasis drug Brinsupri is on the market. The report described the discussions as preliminary, and both companies declined to comment (Fierce Pharma, 11/19).

Cardiff OncologyCRDF +17.4%+$0.01B

Rose 17% on November 16 after the FDA granted Fast Track designation to onvansertib combined with FOLFIRI and bevacizumab as a first-line treatment for RAS-mutated metastatic colorectal cancer (OncLive, 11/16). It held the gain through a week in which the micro-cap biotech median fell 14%.

Laggards

Summit TherapeuticsSMMT −29.7%−$3.4B

Fell 17% on November 16 and another 10% on November 17 after disclosing on Sunday, November 15, that the FDA had issued a complete response letter for ivonescimab with platinum chemotherapy in EGFR-mutated non-small cell lung cancer after EGFR TKI therapy. The company said the FDA cited the lack of a statistically significant overall survival benefit in the HARMONi trial (Summit release, 11/15), and trade press reported that the agency asked for a new global trial (Fierce Biotech).

QuanterixQTRX −33.1%−$0.05B

Fell 28% on November 18 after announcing that CEO Everett Cunningham had stepped down, effective immediately, with Chief Commercial Officer Jim Gute named interim CEO. The company reaffirmed the financial outlook it gave on November 9 (Quanterix 8-K, 11/18).

Microbot MedicalMBOT −30.5%−$0.02B

Fell 34% on November 16 after filing its third-quarter 10-Q, which reported revenue of $72,000, down from $241,000 in the second quarter (Microbot 8-K, 11/16).

04 · Strategic Themes

The Week's Narrative

The Technology Sell-Off Reaches a Correction, and Biotech Follows

In our last edition we described technology stocks cooling and called healthcare's link to them an open question. The decline has since deepened. The S&P 500 fell 8.6% to 6,910 and the Nasdaq Composite 10.0%, with the S&P 500 now more than 10% below its October 6 record (The Closing Print, 11/20). The selling was led by AI and semiconductor stocks (AP, 11/17), and it followed Nvidia's announcement on November 12 that Jensen Huang, its CEO for 33 years, had resigned effective immediately, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO (Nvidia 8-K, 11/12). Biotech indexes fell less than the broad market, but the median biotech fell further than the S&P 500, and the micro-cap median further than the Nasdaq.

The rate picture runs the other way from the usual story. In October we tested whether biotech trades as a rates proxy. This week Treasury yields fell, which on that view should have helped the sector, and biotech still sold off. Investors moved toward bonds as a haven (Bloomberg, 11/20).

CSO's TakeThis week biotech moved with overall risk appetite, not with rates or its own news flow. The indexes, which are weighted toward larger companies, fell less than the S&P 500, while the typical small company fell more. That is the same split by size we have described through the fall, now inside a broad market correction.

Commercial Stage Holds Up Again, Above the Micro-Cap Tier

In the two previous editions, commercial-stage biotechs did not outperform development-stage peers, which complicated the rotation toward commercial names we had described earlier this fall. This week the gap returned. Among small caps the commercial-stage median fell 6.5% against 11.0% for development-stage companies, and among mid/large caps 5.6% against 10.2%. Among micro caps there was no gap: both groups fell about 14%. Across the full biotech universe, the commercial-stage median fell 6.5% and the development-stage median 11.9%.

Buyers were active on both ends of the stage spectrum. Insmed, which markets Brinsupri, rose on the report of Sanofi's interest, and Lilly agreed to acquire Calytrix Bio, a preclinical company working on a myeloproliferative neoplasm program, for up to $1.5B (Fierce Biotech, 11/16).

CSO's TakeIn a broad sell-off, investors in larger biotechs separated companies with product revenue from those without it, while the smallest companies fell together regardless of stage. One week does not establish a trend after two weeks that pointed the other way, so we will keep tracking the stage split within each size tier.
